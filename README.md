**Genre:** Dark romance fantasy · YA · mythology-based · slow-burn
**Language:** English
**Length:** ~30,800 words · 19 chapters + epilogue

In a hidden layer of the modern world built over marble ruins, the Roman pantheon never left — they simply adapted. Jupiter rules through foundations and favors. Hercules hunts threats to the throne. Selene, a seventeen-year-old Oracle, is the door the god speaks through, whether she chooses it or not.

When a prophecy surfaces naming the exiled god Pluto's son as the next turn of the succession wheel, Selene is sent to find him. Instead she dreams of him first. Kael Sombra is eighteen, mopping the lobby of a glass tower his own father owns, when a crack in the marble floor opens into something that has been waiting for him his entire life.

**Themes:** The cost of loyalty · the difference between hunger and justice · forbidden attraction rooted in structural conflict · what it means to be chosen versus what it means to choose

### 2. La Casa Que Respira
`La_Casa_Que_Respira.epub`

**Genre:** Latin American Gothic · folk horror · psychological dread
**Language:** Spanish / English (bilingual prose)
**Length:** ~7,200 words · 5 chapters + epilogue

*La Casa Que Respira* is a cross-adaptation of *The Fallen Seed*, rewritten in the Latin American Gothic mode. The Roman pantheon is relocated to the Bogotá financial district. The Oracle's temple becomes an old institution dressed in glass. Kael's marginality is made specific to Colombian urban precarity. The dark fruit's sweetness is the sweetness of guayaba at the edge of going over.

The horror is the same as the original — the cost of loyalty, the hunger that feels like justice — but the register is different: folk animism, colonial echoes, sawtooth dread, and the specific patience of things that have waited in the red Andean dirt for a very long time.

**Craft principles applied (from Horror Novel style guide):**
- **Pacing:** Sawtooth model — tension builds to a peak, exhales briefly, coils again higher than before
- **Culture & Setting:** Localized sensory detail over generic western horror tropes — the cerros, aguardiente, the smell of the lobby floor, Spanish woven into English prose
- **Themes:** Psychological dread rooted in labour exploitation, indigenous animism against Catholic institutional power, the horror of reasonable things

### 3. La Ciudad Que No Duerme
`La_Ciudad_Que_No_Duerme.epub`

**Genre:** YA urban Gothic · psychological horror · Silent Hill-inspired
**Language:** English / Spanish (bilingual prose)
**Length:** ~30,200 words · 19 chapters + prologue & epilogue

*The City That Never Sleeps.* Set in Bogotá thirteen years after the events of *Silent Hill* (2006, dir. Christophe Gans), Sharon Da Silva is twenty-two, living in La Candelaria, working in the Archivo General de la Nación, seeing a therapist, and trying very hard to have ordinary perceptions about ordinary things.

Then the fog comes from the wrong direction.

Then she finds the first of eleven colonial land records — dated 1692, filed in the wrong hemisphere, describing a property in West Virginia — with a margin notation in a handwriting she recognises from the inside of her own childhood.

The horror is not that Silent Hill followed her. The horror is what had to change for that rule to break.

**Themes:** The horror of unreliable perception · being used as a container for other people's purposes · the difference between carrying a god and burning alone · what two halves of the same person owe each other after twenty-three years apart

**Craft principles applied (from Horror Novel style guide):**
- **Pacing:** Each chapter builds and releases on a different frequency — the archive discovery peaks, morning arrives and resets, the apartment door opens harder than the window did
- **Culture & Setting:** La Candelaria's colonial stone, the cerros, *changua*, *aguapanela*, vallenato through a tienda wall, the institutional silence of a national archive after hours
- **Themes:** Colonial record-keeping as the infrastructure of horror; the cult as bureaucracy; therapy as the frame that makes the supernatural legible or illegible depending on who controls the vocabulary

## How the Books Relate

```
The Fallen Seed (YA)
│
├── La Casa Que Respira
│   Cross-adaptation: same story, Latin American Gothic register.
│   The Roman pantheon relocated to Bogotá. The Oracle's temple
│   becomes an old institution dressed in glass. The dark fruit
│   smells like guayaba going over. The horror of reasonable things.
│
└── La Ciudad Que No Duerme
    Silent Hill sequel: Sharon Da Silva, 13 years later, Bogotá.
    The Fallen Seed's themes (hunger, patience, the cost of service)
    run through a different vessel — colonial archives, Andean fog,
    two halves of a girl who has been burning underground.
```

The connective tissue across all three: **inherited arrangements**. With power, with land, with institutions older than you that will be older still when you leave. The question each book asks differently is whether you feed them willingly, or wait for them to take.

## Reading Recommendations

**In thematic order:**

1. *The Fallen Seed (YA)* — establish the world and characters
2. *La Casa Que Respira* — same story, different lens; see what the register change reveals
3. *La Ciudad Que No Duerme* — draws on both registers and asks what happens to the tools when the institution no longer needs them

**Horror-first order:**

1. *La Ciudad Que No Duerme*
2. *La Casa Que Respira*
3. *The Fallen Seed (YA)* — reads differently once you know what Bogotá does to these themes
